Research

Dr.-Ing. candidate · TU Dresden

From experiment to service

Operational questions applied to quantum links.

The physics of quantum communication is ahead of its operations. Entanglement can be distributed and keys can be exchanged, but mostly under controlled conditions and by the people who built the apparatus. My research asks the questions a network operator would ask next: how a quantum resource is provisioned, how its quality is measured and its degradation detected, how it is allocated when demand exceeds supply, and what guarantees can be attached to it.

I approach these questions from the systems side, through the engineering built for QD-CamNetz: resource orchestration, verification workflows and the timing infrastructure that deterministic services depend on. I treat the quantum layer as a component like any other, with an interface, a failure model and a cost.

The other half of the question is what the resource is used for. My test case is federated learning across a fibre mesh, with keys drawn from the same entanglement source.

Themes · Four threads

Lines of inquiry

Entanglement as an allocatable resource

Entanglement is consumed by use, degrades in storage and cannot be copied. No classical resource management model accounts for those properties. This thread looks at how quantum-classical networks should use and share entanglement, and how its scarcity changes the behaviour of the parties that depend on it.

Security and randomness as network services

A network that produces quantum randomness and quantum-secured keys gains new service classes, and with them new engineering questions. These include efficient error-rate estimation in quantum key distribution, quantum entropy pools with provable properties for cryptographic applications, and quantum-resistant architectures for constrained IoT devices that will never terminate a quantum link of their own.

Privacy-preserving federated learning

Training data stays local, but gradients cross the network, and that traffic is what a harvest-now-decrypt-later adversary records today. Entanglement-derived keys mask the gradients in secure aggregation. Benchmarked against state-of-the-art methods in full-stack simulation, the pipeline withstands harvest-now-decrypt-later and eavesdropping attacks that break the classical baseline. Open questions are throughput, scheduling cost and which guarantees hold on an operational network.

Time synchronization

Every guarantee in the other threads depends on synchronization. This thread covers the design tradeoffs of quantum time synchronization for industrial networks, and the entanglement-derived mechanisms, at picosecond precision in simulation, that let a distributed testbed behave as a single deterministic system. Three patent applications filed with Deutsche Telekom T-Labs take that result toward GNSS-independent timing for coherent joint transmission in 5G-Advanced and 6G radio networks.

Software · Simulation code

qd-entanglement-fl

Code from the federated learning research.

Simulator in which quantum-dot Bell pairs over a campus fibre mesh provision the pairwise masks of Bonawitz-style secure aggregation, with BBM92 as the default protocol framing. It models source characterization, sifting, reconciliation and privacy amplification, mesh topology, hierarchical aggregation with dropout recovery, and classical baselines for comparison.

Software · Applied output

open5gs-qrandom-mod

Software from the randomness research.

A modified build of Open5GS, a widely used open source 5G core, that takes its entropy from quantum random number generation. It answers a practical question: if the network produces quantum randomness, where does standard core-network software actually consume it?
